**Station 3: Bill of Rights \(Jacob & CJ\) - v0.0.0-alpha**** **

The 1st Amendment: Speech, Religion, Press, Assembly, and Petition.

The 4th Amendment: Protection against unreasonable search and seizure.

Due Process: Basics of the 5th and 6th Amendments \(rights of the accused\).

Protection of Rights: Why these ten were added to limit government power.

Rights vs. Responsibilities: Balancing personal freedom with respecting others.



### **I. Fundamentals of the Bill of Rights **

**Discuss with the Scouts: **

**Definition:** What exactly is an "Amendment" and why did the Founders feel the Constitution was "unfinished" without these first ten?

**Purpose:** Explain the role of the Bill of Rights in protecting the individual from excessive government power.

**The Anti-Federalists:** Briefly mention the group of people who refused to sign the Constitution until these rights were added.

### **II. The First Amendment \(The Core Freedoms\) **

**Explain and discuss each of the five freedoms guaranteed: **

**Religion:** The government cannot set a national religion or stop you from practicing yours.

**Speech:** The right to express opinions \(with common-sense safety limits\).

**Press:** The right for news to report the truth.

**Assembly:** The right to gather peacefully in a group \(like a Scout meeting\).

**Petition:** The right to ask the government to fix a problem or change a law.

### **III. Protection of Rights \(Amendments 2?4\) **

**Review the following protections with the patrol: **

**2nd Amendment:** The right of the people to keep and bear arms.

**3rd Amendment:** The restriction on "quartering" soldiers in private homes without consent.

**4th Amendment:** Define "unreasonable search and seizure." Discuss what "probable cause" means and why privacy is a protected right.

### **IV. Legal & Court Protections \(Amendments 5?8\) **

**Discuss how the law must treat a citizen accused of a crime: **

**5th Amendment:** Explain **Due Process**, the protection against **Double Jeopardy**, and the right to remain silent \(self-incrimination\).

**6th Amendment:** The right to a **speedy and public trial** by an impartial jury, and the right to have a lawyer.

**7th Amendment:** The right to a jury trial in civil \(non-criminal\) cases.

**8th Amendment:** The protection against **excessive bail** and **cruel and unusual punishment**.

### **V. Reserved Powers \(Amendments 9?10\) **

**Explain the "Catch-all" protections: **

**9th Amendment:** Discuss why the Founders stated that just because a right isn't listed in the Constitution doesn't mean the people don't have it.

**10th Amendment:** Explain that any power not specifically given to the federal government belongs to the **States** or the **People**.

### **VI. Practical Application **

**Have the Scouts demonstrate understanding: **

Give the Scouts **three scenarios** of government overreach and have them identify which of the 10 Amendments would be used to stop it.

Discuss how the **Scout Law** \(specifically being Brave and Loyal\) relates to standing up for these rights.
